`ColorPicker` chooses a colour from swatches, from three sliders, or by typing
its hex. Try all three:

<UiExample source={source} file="ColorPickerDemo.tsx">
  <ColorPickerDemo client:visible />
</UiExample>

- **`value`** and **`onChange`** hold a hex string, `#rrggbb`. With **`alpha`**
  it is `#rrggbbaa` and there is an opacity slider.
- **Three sliders, not a square.** There is no accessible way to move one
  control in two directions at once, so hue, saturation and lightness are
  three ordinary sliders. Each says its value in words: "Hue, 217 degrees,
  blue".
- **A colour is read with a name and its hex**, such as "blue, #3b82f6", so
  the hex is not six digits of nothing.
- **`presets`** are swatches: a radio group that is one tab stop, moved through
  with the arrow keys.
- **`showEyeDropper`** adds a button that picks a colour from the screen, in
  browsers that have the EyeDropper API.
- **The slider markers choose a light or dark border for each track's colour.**
  An opposite-colour outer edge keeps the marker visible across gradients and
  when opacity reveals the surface behind it. These use
  `hozo-marker-on-light` and `hozo-marker-on-dark`, whose polarity stays the same
  in both page themes. Keep their contrast when customizing the palette.
- **`thumbLook({ track, color })`** customizes the value-dependent classes by
  returning `{ className }`. `track` is `hue`, `saturation`, `lightness` or
  `alpha`; `color` is a hex sample of that track at its slider value, including
  opacity for `alpha`. The picker cannot infer the surface behind transparent
  colours. Keep ordinary focus styling in `thumbClassName`: each slider owns
  its own keyboard focus.
